Section 1

Overview & Core Concept

AuraCast incorporates an on-demand machine learning pipeline that forecasts local weather behavior directly from recent historical observations. Rather than applying a single universal model trained on static external datasets, AuraCast trains a customized model on the fly for the exact geographic coordinates requested by the user.

What the Model Does

Ingests the past 30 days of hourly climate observations for a location, extracts cyclical diurnal patterns, atmospheric pressure tendencies, and lagged momentum features, and computes:

  • 24-Hour Rain Probability: Calibrated risk percentage (0–100%) and risk tier.
  • 6-Hour Meteorological Trajectory: Predicted hourly temperature and humidity.
  • Model Validation Report: Real-time validation metrics on held-out test data.

Observation vs. Prediction

It is critical to distinguish between the two distinct data streams in AuraCast:

  • Current Weather (OpenWeather): Live instantaneous physical telemetry (thermometers, barometers, radar).
  • Predictive ML (Scikit-Learn): Statistical estimators trained on 30-day temporal lag relationships.
Section 2

Data Sources & Ingestion

The application relies on two decoupled meteorological APIs, each serving a dedicated purpose in the progressive loading architecture:

OpenWeather API Live Telemetry

Provides instantaneous real-time current weather metrics. Delivers data in ~150ms to populate the top Hero section and Atmospheric indicators without blocking on ML processing.

• Temperature & Feels-Like
• Humidity & Atmospheric Pressure
• Wind Speed, Gusts & Direction
• Cloud Cover & Visibility
• Astronomical Sunrise & Sunset
Open-Meteo Archive API Training Dataset

Retrieves 30 days (720 hourly chronological records) of local weather history for the exact coordinate pair. This dataset serves as the training corpus for the Random Forest pipeline.

• Hourly 2m Temperature (`temperature_2m`)
• Relative Humidity (`relative_humidity_2m`)
• Surface Pressure (`surface_pressure`)
• Total Precipitation & Rain (`precipitation`, `rain`)
• Wind Speed & Wind Direction (10m)
Section 3

Data Preprocessing & Validation

Implemented in app/ml/preprocessing.py, raw time-series data undergoes strict data-cleaning rules before being passed to feature engineering:

1. Chronological Sorting

Timestamps are converted to native pandas datetimes and sorted chronologically to maintain time-series integrity.

2. Gap Imputation

Forward-fill (ffill) followed by backward-fill (bfill) ensures continuous sensor timelines without dropped hours.

3. Physical Bounds Clipping

Relative humidity is bounded strictly to $[0, 100]\%$; precipitation and wind speeds are clamped at $\ge 0.0$.

4. Target Generation

Constructs 24h rolling forward precipitation target and 6 multi-horizon future targets ($+1\text{h}$ to $+6\text{h}$).

// Target Definition in preprocessing.py
rolling_forward_precip = df["precipitation"].iloc[::-1].rolling(window=24, min_periods=1).sum().iloc[::-1]
df["target_rain_next_24h"] = (rolling_forward_precip > 0.2).astype(int)
for h in range(1, 7):
df[f"target_temp_h{h}"] = df["temperature"].shift(-h)
df[f"target_hum_h{h}"] = df["humidity"].shift(-h)
Section 4

Feature Engineering Matrix

Raw meteorological readings are transformed into 30 structured feature columns in app/ml/features.py to provide the model with historical momentum, cyclical timing, and physical atmospheric relationships:

Cyclical Time Signals

Converts standard hour-of-day and month-of-year into continuous trigonometric signals so the model recognizes that 23:00 and 00:00 are adjacent:

sin_hour = sin(2π × hour / 24)
cos_hour = cos(2π × hour / 24)
sin_month = sin(2π × (month - 1) / 12)
cos_month = cos(2π × (month - 1) / 12)
Physical & Vector Features

Extracts thermodynamic dew point approximations and decomposes polar wind vectors into orthogonal East-West ($U$) and North-South ($V$) components:

dew_point = temp - ((100 - humidity) / 5)
wind_u = -speed × sin(radians(direction))
wind_v = -speed × cos(radians(direction))
Time-Lag Features (Inertia)

Injects past observations to capture thermal and moisture inertia over preceding hours:

temp_lag_1h, temp_lag_2h, temp_lag_3h, temp_lag_6h
hum_lag_1h, hum_lag_2h, hum_lag_3h, hum_lag_6h
press_lag_1h, press_lag_3h, precip_lag_1h
Rolling Aggregations & Pressure Trend

Quantifies short-term trends and volatility. Crucially, computes the 3-hour barometric pressure drop ($\Delta P_{3h}$), a primary sign of incoming storm fronts:

temp_roll_mean_6h, temp_roll_std_6h, temp_roll_mean_24h
hum_roll_mean_6h, hum_roll_mean_24h
press_trend_3h = pressure - pressure(t - 3h)
precip_accum_24h = sum(precipitation, 24h)
Section 5

Machine Learning Models & Algorithms

AuraCast utilizes Ensemble Random Forest architectures implemented with Scikit-Learn (app/ml/models.py). Random Forests construct an ensemble of randomized decision trees that vote (for classification) or average (for regression), drastically mitigating overfitting:

Precipitation Classifier: RandomForestClassifier

Binary Probability

Trained to predict whether total precipitation over the next 24-hour window will exceed $0.2\text{ mm}$. Uses balanced class weighting (class_weight="balanced") so that dry seasons with rare rainfall events are not ignored by the loss function. Computes continuous probability via predict_proba(X).

n_estimators: 45
max_depth: 6
min_samples_split: 4
class_weight: balanced

Multi-Horizon Hourly Regressors: RandomForestRegressor (×12)

Direct Multi-Step Forecasting

Rather than compounding error through recursive autoregression, AuraCast fits independent direct-horizon estimators for each subsequent hour ($+1\text{h}, +2\text{h}, \dots, +6\text{h}$) across both temperature and humidity.

6× Temperature Models

Separate tree ensembles fitted to predict exact Celsius temperature at target horizons $h \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.

6× Humidity Models

Separate tree ensembles predicting relative humidity percentages clipped to $[0, 100]\%$ at target horizons $h \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$.

Section 6

Step-by-Step Prediction Lifecycle

What happens from the moment a user submits a city search or GPS location:

1
Geographic Coordinate Resolution: The location search endpoint geocodes the query string to high-precision latitude/longitude coordinates.
2
Instant OpenWeather Dispatch (~150ms): Live observation is rendered immediately on the UI. The prediction cards transition to dark-shimmer skeleton states.
3
Historical Telemetry Retrieval: Open-Meteo archive API fetches ~720 hourly records specific to the coordinate pair.
4
Data Cleansing & Feature Construction: Data is sanitized, time-lagged, rolling-window aggregated, and encoded into a 30-column feature matrix.
5
Chronological Validation & Model Fitting: An 80/20 temporal split validates accuracy, followed by tree fitting across all 13 estimators.
6
Inference & Targeted DOM Replacement: The final prediction bundle arrives via /api/prediction, replacing solely the prediction skeletons with live gauges and charts.
Section 7

Interpreting Model Output

24-Hour Rain Probability & Risk Tiers

The precipitation gauge score represents the probability of measurable precipitation ($\ge 0.2\text{ mm}$) over the next 24 hours:

Low Risk (< 20%) Dry conditions expected.
Moderate Risk (20% – 49%) Isolated light showers possible.
High Risk (50% – 79%) High likelihood of sustained rainfall.
Very High Risk (≥ 80%) Imminent or heavy precipitation.

6-Hour Trajectory Strip

Displays predicted temperature, humidity, and condition classification for each upcoming hour ($+1\text{h}$ through $+6\text{h}$). Estimated conditions are derived from the joint distribution of predicted moisture and thermal values:

Rainy Rain prob > 60% & Humidity > 75%
Cloudy Humidity > 70%
Warm / Clear Temperature > 28°C
Clear Standard baseline
Section 8

Scientific Model Limitations

Meteorological modeling involves chaotic fluid dynamics and complex boundary conditions. For complete transparency, users should understand these constraints:

Historical Data Quality

Predictions are bounded by the observational fidelity and station density of Open-Meteo reanalysis data for the selected coordinates.

Sudden Extreme Convective Events

Microbursts, isolated severe thunderstorms, or rapid frontal collapses can develop faster than a 30-day statistical pattern reflects.

Microclimate & Topography

Urban heat island effects, coastal sea-breeze fronts, and sharp mountain rain shadows may produce localized variance.

No Future Guarantee

Statistical patterns from the past 30 days do not guarantee future weather; atmospheric shifts can alter localized dynamics.

Horizon Degradation

Prediction uncertainty increases as the forecast horizon extends from $+1\text{h}$ toward $+6\text{h}$ and beyond.

Upstream API Availability

If the historical archive API is unreachable, ML predictions gracefully fail while live OpenWeather observations remain usable.

Section 9

Real-Time Validation Metrics

Every time a model is fitted, it evaluates itself on held-out observations using a strict 80/20 Chronological Split. This avoids lookahead bias (training on future samples to predict past samples):

Regression Metrics (Temperature & Humidity)

MAE (Mean Absolute Error) |y - ŷ|

Average magnitude of errors in raw physical units (°C or % relative humidity).

RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) √((y - ŷ)²)

Penalizes large outlier forecast errors more heavily than small deviations.

Classification Metrics (Rain Occurrence)

Accuracy & F1-Score Harmonic Mean

F1-score measures harmonic mean of precision and recall to assess performance on unbalanced rain days.

Precision & Recall TP / (TP + FP)

Evaluates false alarm rate vs. missed rainfall detection rates.
